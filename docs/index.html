Somebody is always there

Describing an image requires not only recognizing what is visible but also deciding what should be inferred. We study a systematic gap between humans and vision-language models (VLMs): VLMs attribute visible actions or events to people who are not present in the image. We call this unwarranted person attribution bias. Across 22 open VLMs, captions of person-absent scenes mention a person four times as often as human-written captions of the same images.

“Make your best guess of what might be happening in this scene in one sentence.”
VLM

“Someone is relaxing with a book and a beer.”

Human

“A book and beer are on a restaurant table.”

In a preference study, human judges and VLM judges both prefer captions that avoid such attributions. That makes a simple remedy possible: a VLM's own judgement can supply the preference signal, without new human labels.

Dataset

NOBODY: VLMs judge their own captions

Eight VLMs caption the same person-free photographs twice, in separate chats: as usual, and under the instruction “Do not mention any people.” Each model then judges its own two captions in both presentation orders. Pairs with agreeing verdicts form NOBODY, on which we align VLMs through direct preference optimization (DPO). A quarter of the pairs come from photographs that do show people, so aligned models keep describing visible people.

Benchmark

NOBODY-Bench: agreement with human captions and human judges

NOBODY-Bench scores one greedy caption per image against the human captions and human judges of the same images, in seconds and from text alone. A model can lower its rate of person mentions by never mentioning people, so agreement is measured separately on images without and with a visible person.

PAMA

Person-Absent Mention Agreement

Probability that a randomly drawn human caption makes the same mention decision as the model, averaged over 664 images without a visible person.

PPMA

Person-Present Mention Agreement

The same agreement over 949 images with a visible person. It guards against a model that simply stops mentioning people.

PJA

Public-Judge Agreement

Agreement with the human judges of four public preference datasets on 3,626 images, half of which they preferred described without a person.

Results

One dataset, 22 VLMs

After alignment on NOBODY, agreement with human captions on person-absent images (PAMA) rises for all 22 VLMs, curators and held-out models alike, and reaches or exceeds inter-human agreement (82.8%) for 18 of them, while agreement on person-present images (PPMA) changes by about one point at most.
